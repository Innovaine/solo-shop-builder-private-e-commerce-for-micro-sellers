# WHO PAYS — Solo Shop Builder

Let me be direct: this idea has a real buyer. But you've named them wrong, and that's costing you clarity on whether it actually works.

---

## 1. THE EXACT BUYER

**Not:** "Micro-sellers" or "Etsy resellers." Too broad. I've watched us chase this ghost three times.

**Actually:** A **full-time Etsy reseller or local craftsperson doing $15K–$80K annual revenue, running 1–3 product lines, currently managing orders across Etsy + email + spreadsheet + manual payment tracking**, who:
- Pays Etsy 6.5% + 3% + $0.20 per transaction (losing ~10% of margin to fees)
- Spends 3–5 hours/week on order admin (reminders, shipping label generation, customer follow-ups)
- Already has a website *instinct* (wants a branded URL) but not a website *budget* ($2K/year is genuinely money she'd rather not spend)
- Has had a customer ask "do you have your own site?" and felt friction

**Company size:** Solo or 1–2 part-time helpers (not a "team" with budget committees).

**Budget they pull from:** Personal operating margin, not a departmental P&L. She's deciding between "invest in this" vs. "take home $200 that month."

**The hard part:** This buyer exists. But she's not a "SaaS buyer." She thinks in terms of "does this pay for itself" not "does this multiply my throughput."

---

## 2. WHY THEY PAY NOW

**On fire, this quarter:**
- Etsy algorithm changes (March 2024 forward) killed organic reach for many resellers. Revenue pressure is real and sudden.
- Holiday season prep (if we're in Sept–Nov) or tax anxiety (if Jan–Mar). Both create urgency to consolidate and reduce dependencies.
- One recent customer complaint: "Why don't you have your own site?" hit her harder than it should have.

**The trap:** "Now" is not when she's making SaaS decisions. She's making them when her Etsy revenue dips or when she hires her first helper and realizes she needs a system. That's usually 6–12 months after the pain starts.

---

## 3. CLOSEST COMPETITOR / CURRENT ALTERNATIVE

She's not comparing you to Shopify ($30/month + theme costs + payment processing fees ≈ $70/month).

**She's comparing you to:**
- Doing nothing and taking the Etsy fees
- A free Wix or Squarespace site that she abandoned after realizing she'd need to manually sync orders anyway
- Asking her brother-in-law to build her a site (free, broken, good enough)

**The real competitor:** Status quo. Not because the pain isn't real, but because the switching cost (learn new platform, migrate product catalog, retrain customers where to buy) is higher than the Etsy fee pain.

The second-strongest competitor is **Shopify** — but only if she ever thinks of herself as a "business" rather than a "side thing." Most don't yet.

---

## 4. THE ONE NUMBER

For this to hit $1M ARR at $20/month (the price point where a micro-seller says "okay"):

**50,000 paying sellers × $20/month × 12 months = $12M ARR**

You need **$1M ARR**, so:

**~4,200 paying sellers × $20/month × 12 = $1.008M ARR**

The multiplication that has to be true:
- **TAM:** ~500K full-time Etsy resellers in the US + EU doing >$15K annual revenue = real denominator
- **Adoption:** 0.85% of that TAM pays you by year 2 (4,200 / 500K)
- **Pricing:** $20/month (she has to believe it beats the Etsy fee savings or brings in enough new customers to justify)

**Where this breaks:** 0.85% adoption assumes:
1. You can actually reach her (she doesn't read SaaS blogs or Product Hunt)
2. She can quantify her Etsy fee savings (most can't, haven't tracked it)
3. Switching costs are low (they're not — she has reviews, customer history, search rank on Etsy)
4. Payment processing is solved and obvious (not for micro-sellers who hate complexity)

---

## 5. THIS WEEK'S VALIDATION TEST

**Call 5 full-time Etsy resellers doing $20K–$60K annual revenue.**

You already know 3–5 who "pre-committed" to testing this. Call them. But call them *after* you ask these people:

**Who to find:** 
- Etsy sellers with >500 all-time sales and >4.8-star rating (signal of seriousness)
- Search Etsy for categories like vintage, handmade jewelry, home decor. Find the power sellers (>1000 sales). Email 5 asking for 20 min.
- OR: Find Reddit threads in r/Etsy, r/AmazonFBA where sellers complain about fees. DM the complainers.

**The one question:**
> "Last month, how much of your revenue went to Etsy's fees—percentage-wise? And if I could cut that by 30%, what would you do with that money?"

**What kills the idea:**
- She doesn't know her fee percentage (means she's not tracking unit economics; you can't sell her on savings she can't see)
- She says "put it back into advertising on Etsy" (means she's locked into the Etsy moat, not looking to escape)
- She says "I'd need to see how many customers I'd actually lose" (switching cost is the real blocker, not fees)
- None of the 5 have a website or have tried one (means branded URL desire isn't real, just conversation fodder)

**What *validates* the idea:**
- She knows her fee percentage within 1% (means she's financially mature enough to be a SaaS customer)
- She says "I'd reinvest it in ads off-platform" or "I'd hire someone part-time" (means she's capital-constrained, not margin-constrained; she'd pay for relief)
- She's already tried a Shopify/Wix site and abandoned it specifically because syncing orders was painful (means you're solving a named problem, not creating a category)
- 3+ of 5 have sent a customer to their own site or Instagram shop when Etsy was down (means the behavior already exists)

**Timeline:** Call by EOD Thursday. By Friday, you'll know if this buyer is real or if we're chasing sentiment.

## MY READ

This idea is better than the last four because:
- The buyer actually exists (unlike "mid-market finance teams" that don't have this problem)
- There's a pre-committed test group (not hypothetical)
- Single-tenant unit economics are defensible at $20/month with 3-5 customers shipping real orders

**But** — and this is important — you're conflating "the buyer exists" with "the buyer will pay." She exists. She might not be the customer. The pre-committed sellers are at risk of being helpful rather than representative. They're invested in you succeeding (maybe they're friends, maybe they're betting on you for other reasons). Test with strangers first.

**The real risk:** You'll ship a working product, get 5 sellers paying, think you've validated the idea, and hit a wall at 15 because the 6th seller who's not emotionally invested will tell you what she actually wants. Frontload that conversation.
